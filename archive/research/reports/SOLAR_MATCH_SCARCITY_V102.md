# Where the frozen matcher runs out of correspondences

The 192 positive rows reduce to 128 distinct camera and query conditions across eight development terrains. The two reference-render baselines produced identical saved outputs whenever only the nominal reference-light row changed. Each physical camera was measured at four query-light indices.

- **The main failure occurred before pose estimation.** Of 39 full-catalogue conditions without a pose, 38 never reached six pairs in any template. Twenty-five had no pairs. The remaining condition reached six and still failed. Twenty of the 38 scarce conditions had at least 230 of 256 geometrically available patch centres. Median geometric availability was 231 for scarce conditions and 242 for returned poses.
- **Weak image structure affected both sides.** The scarce group had median 95th-percentile grayscale code 2 in both the query and the reference-view render at the matching light. The group with at least six pairs had medians 71 and 69. Of the 38 scarce conditions, 21 had both sides at or below code 3, six had only the query that low, and two had only the template that low. The two image gradient ranks were strongly associated with one another (0.946). The descriptive associations between maximum candidate count and query/template gradients were 0.846/0.846 within camera and 0.448/0.362 within terrain and query light. Geometric crop availability was also associated with count within the latter strata (0.336). These data cannot isolate one causal side.
- **Bright pixels alone did not ensure useful texture.** Eight of nine bright but scarce conditions belonged to np31-r0-c7 at query lights 16 and 17. Across the same four cameras, the best candidate contained 0–4 pairs at those lights and 19–27 at light 0. The fraction of co-visible 25-pixel windows with mean adjacent contrast of at least one code in both views fell from 53–69% to 6–26%, even though the global upper grayscale quantile rose. The camera, terrain and geometric window count were fixed within each comparison.
- **Matching the illumination is a separate lever.** Adding the omitted odd reference templates corrected 14 of 64 odd-query conditions with zero losses. All 14 successes selected the newly available exact-light reference render, and their median query/template 95th percentiles were 76.5/73. The full catalogue still produced six or more candidate pairs in 40 of 128 deduplicated nonoverlap controls, although neither catalogue returned a pose on these controls.
- **Raw matches do not establish improvement.** With query light 17 fixed, switching the original reference illumination from light 0 to 8 gave MatchAnything–EfficientLoFTR a median of 53.5 additional proposed pairs, a median gain of zero correct two-pixel matches, and zero net correct poses. With the original light-16 reference fixed, changing the query from light 16 to 1 removed a median of 18.5 correct two-pixel matches for SIFT and 1,900.5 for MatchAnything–EfficientLoFTR. That comparison changes query appearance and illumination agreement together.

The already specified 24-positive radiometric cohort contains eight conditions below six pairs. Two are bright on both sides, four have both sides at code 2, and two have a query at code 2 with a brighter exact-light reference. A matched two-arm normalization trial can measure a joint effect. If that treatment recovers credible correspondences, independently switching query and template normalization would identify which side contributes. Template availability and patch texture within geometric overlap should remain separate reporting strata.

This audit uses immutable saved results, original PNG codes and existing geometric labels. It opens no matcher or pose estimator. The exact-light reference template and correspondence labels serve retrospective measurement only. The results describe repeated views of eight observed terrains.
