# Two closer comparisons for lunar localization

The closest completed comparison on our exact development bank remains 114/192 correct raw poses for direct relighting versus 42/192 for MatchAnything–EfficientLoFTR. This uses common absolute-pose scoring on eight simulated terrains. Direct relighting draws from a prepared reference library. The newer studies below add mission-relevant comparisons that use different information and metrics. [Matched development results](SOLAR_FULL_PAIRED_DEVELOPMENT_V94.md)

## Absolute localization against an orbital map

The peer-reviewed 2026 Endurance trade study by Bhamidipati and colleagues compares LunarNav, ShadowNav and Censible in 882 simulated scenarios. These systems use stationary 360-degree rover stereo panoramas against orbital references. The design varies DEM, location, solar elevation and how stereo depth is obtained. Its primary result concerns metric positioning under those inputs. The 882 scenarios repeat locations and terrain models. The trade study does not yield a leaderboard for our single-pair six-degree-of-freedom problem. [ION proceedings](https://doi.org/10.33012/2026.20806) · [Study design](https://www.ion.org/gnss/abstracts.cfm?paperID=17061)

Adnan Ansar coauthored the original Censible work. Its modified census transform matches rover panoramas to orbital references and reports sub-meter accuracy on 264 actual Mars panoramas. That operational result makes intensity-based and ordinal matching important context for our low-contrast work. Its image geometry, auxiliary information and sensor observations differ from our simulated lunar pairs. [Original JPL paper](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/2024_Global_Localization_ICRA.pdf)

## Real testbed behavior under changing lighting

The October 2026 Casadesus Vila and Gao preprint compares eleven detector–matcher combinations on nine datasets. On LunaPolaris pairs with the same pose geometry across lighting conditions, classical detectors benefited from spotlight illumination. Their SuperPoint combinations showed no resolved change under the same comparison. The authors also found that relative rankings on synthetic data differed from rankings on real testbeds. Their experiments use relative pose AUC and visual odometry rather than absolute reference-map pose at 2 degrees and 1% reference range. They leave detector-free methods such as RoMa out of the study. [Preprint, sections III–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30774v1)

The useful transfer target here is a contrast mechanism with a common independently tracked camera geometry. LunaPolaris has motion capture, LiDAR and repeated camera paths with different physical lighting. The authors use sequence 01 for ceiling lighting, 07 for a low spotlight and 10 for spotlight plus headlight. Motion-capture interpolation and hand-eye calibration still contribute reference uncertainty. LunaPolaris and our prepared SPICE-HL3 study share the University of Luxembourg LunaLab facility, so a second sensor collection there would add lighting and hardware diversity without adding another site. [LunaPolaris source paper](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/67131) · [SPICE-HL3 transfer preparation](SPICE_HL3_TRANSFER_PREPARATION_V100.md)

## Bounded data access check

The publisher's metadata for sequences [01](https://zenodo.org/records/15737938), [07](https://zenodo.org/records/15745932) and [10](https://zenodo.org/records/15747096) lists CC BY 4.0 SQLite ROS bags of 33.83 GB, 29.70 GB and 34.11 GB respectively, with publisher checksums. A range request for bytes 0–99 returned HTTP 206 with the exact advertised file size for each bag. All three returned a valid SQLite header and 4,096-byte pages. No ETag was present on those responses. This establishes small-range access to the file headers. The exact camera topic, calibrated frame alignment, bounded page fetch and independently valid co-visible geometry still need qualification before selecting optical frames. No image message or matcher was opened.

The direct relighting and exposure pilots still need their original map and reference-light access recorded alongside outcomes. A simulation gain would motivate a transfer experiment with measured pose and physical lighting variation. Neither the rover papers' figures nor these data-access checks supply a matched accuracy number for our method.
