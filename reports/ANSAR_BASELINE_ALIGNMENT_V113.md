# Aligning our lunar pose benchmark with Ansar's results

## Published correspondence and pose evidence

Rothenberger, Georgakis, Cheng and Ansar evaluate lunar-trained LoFTR on synthetic south-pole orthographic imagery. In Table 1, 0E is the held-out geographic region. RA-1 and RA-5 are the proportions of returned correspondences with reprojection error below one and five pixels, respectively. The mean match count includes all returned matches. [2025 AIAA paper, author PDF, Table 1](https://drive.google.com/file/d/10O-6Q9ujkxPZ8bVEY5JarR7Hg7t2dSRb/view?usp=sharing) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2025-2073)

| Method on unseen region 0E | RA-1 | RA-5 | Mean returned matches |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SIFT | 13.8% | 20.2% | 33.6 |
| Generic LoFTR | 20.8% | 39.1% | 742.9 |
| Lunar-trained LoFTR | 70.6% | 85.9% | 1,495.6 |

Lunar training improved RA-1 by 49.8 percentage points over generic LoFTR while also producing about twice as many matches in the held-out region. This motivates a lunar-trained correspondence baseline and a separate pose test. Our denominator is all eligible image pairs, including failures. A correct absolute camera pose requires both rotation error ≤2° and translation error ≤1% of the median reference camera-to-surface range. The real LROC examples in the AIAA paper provide qualitative transfer evidence. [Author PDF, Sections IV-C and V](https://drive.google.com/file/d/10O-6Q9ujkxPZ8bVEY5JarR7Hg7t2dSRb/view?usp=sharing) · [Our pose criterion and same-worker results](SOLAR_MODERN_ARM_PILOT_V110.md)

The 2024 Space Imaging Workshop paper already tests simultaneous perspective and lighting changes. Georgakis and Ansar used a 70 × 70 km lunar south-pole DEM sampled at 10 m, two simulated cameras at 3 km altitude with 100° horizontal fields of view, and translations up to 500 m. PerspA has 460 pairs within the training attitude range of up to 15° pitch and roll. PerspB has 507 pairs and extends pitch and roll up to 45°. Figures 6 and 7 evaluate correspondence reprojection across illumination offsets. A comparable pose experiment would run those correspondences through known reference geometry and a common pose solver. [2024 SIW paper, perspective dataset and Figures 6–7](https://seal.ae.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/2024-09/GeorgakisGeorgios.pdf)

## What our LIMA-inspired control measured

The 2025 paper has a separate correlation method called LIMA. It uses two co-registered references and the known Sun vectors for all three images to construct a composite and search for alignment by terrain slope coherence. The paper's orthoprojected correlation setup and the separate lunar-trained LoFTR experiment answer different questions. Our implementation isolates horizontal Lambertian synthesis and feeds the template to SIFT and the common pose solver. This tests the effect of the synthesis component under supplied illumination. [Author PDF, Sections III and IV](https://drive.google.com/file/d/10O-6Q9ujkxPZ8bVEY5JarR7Hg7t2dSRb/view?usp=sharing) · [Frozen supplied-Sun protocol and results](SOLAR_SUPPLIED_SUN_POSE_PILOT_V102.md)

- **Same camera, v103.** Of 384 scheduled odd-light reference conditions, 352 had a valid angular bracket and 342 had informative windows for the primary comparison. Synthesis improved local signed-gradient error in 289 of the 342 scored conditions. The median fell from 1.091 to 1.010 grayscale codes. The reference viewpoint was fixed throughout. [Photometry report and saved results](SOLAR_REFERENCE_PHOTOMETRY_V103.md)
- **Changed camera, v102.** Nearest-even and two-reference synthesis each recovered 16/24 correct raw positive poses and 10/16 on withheld odd lights. Synthesis gained one case and lost one. The paired terrain interval for the net overall gain was −12.50 to +12.50 percentage points. Both arms returned zero raw nonoverlap poses over 24 cases, including 14 where both arms had angular support. Synthesis lacked a valid bracket in the other ten. These are raw outputs from the supplied-Sun experiment. [Pose report and independent replay](SOLAR_SUPPLIED_SUN_POSE_PILOT_V102.md)

The same-view photometric gain therefore did not improve net pose recovery in the measured changed-view experiment. The later blind v112 sentry identified a separate bottleneck. Neither renderer replicate contained a correct pose anywhere in its proposal bank. Both atlas arms fell back because common valid support was empty on the positive banks. The two replicates share one positive camera pair. [Blind sentry and sealed metrics](SOLAR_SECONDARY_BLIND_SENTRY_V112.md)

## Geo-LoFTR and the missing comparison

Geo-LoFTR is a close precedent for using reference terrain inside learned matching and then estimating absolute pose with PnP. Its authors use orthographic reference windows and a reference DTM. On 500 simulated Mars queries with nadir pointing, altitudes of 64–200 m and a fixed 10° Sun elevation, their azimuth-offset experiments report 54–63% localization within 1 m. They measure a Geo-LoFTR forward pass at 887 ± 27 ms on a Jetson AGX Xavier. The position threshold, terrain, viewing setup and hardware differ from our joint lunar pose and CPU measurements. The official simulated-Mars Geo-LoFTR weight member has been identified in the archive. Checkpoint byte verification, a pretrained control and a matched lunar run remain open. [Geo-LoFTR paper, Sections III and IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09795v3) · [Official weights archive](https://huggingface.co/dpisanti/mbl-mars/blob/8c66a64949382f0b140ef65e1ae4b3b438fa0d21/weights_loftr.zip) · [Qualification record](solar_geoloftr_metadata_v81.json)

The official evaluation driver uses the query's true pose and depth to centre its default 1 km map crop. A matched blind test should call the model with the same externally fixed map window available to the other methods and keep query depth and pose with the scorer. [Pinned official evaluation driver](https://github.com/nasa-jpl/mbl_mars/blob/e1590f0fe25a7d19b6b22d65c22f9305e46cf868/evaluate_mbl.py#L350-L420)

## Next matched experiment

1. **Qualify the proposal sources.** Pin a lunar-trained LoFTR checkpoint if an exact author artifact can be obtained. Compare it with generic LoFTR, SIFT and the frozen compact control. A new lunar training run would be a separate baseline. For Geo-LoFTR, pin its released checkpoint, verify a real pretrained forward pass and native coordinate conversion on a known-transform fixture, and time the full pipeline on the comparison CPU. Apply the same qualification to a LIMA reproduction before naming it as such. [2025 AIAA author PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/10O-6Q9ujkxPZ8bVEY5JarR7Hg7t2dSRb/view?usp=sharing) · [Geo-LoFTR qualification record](solar_geoloftr_metadata_v81.json)
2. **Fix the information available to every arm.** Start with the four sealed v112 slots to check whether an added proposal source puts a correct pose in both positive banks. Then freeze a larger paired development cohort before inference. Share the same native query pixels, field of view, reference map extent, geometry, pose-search prior and pose solver. Give supplied Sun to every eligible arm in that track. Keep estimated-Sun results separate. A Geo-LoFTR orthographic input or the two LIMA reference views require an explicitly shared reference bundle for that track. Build it from reference-side data and preserve the native perspective comparison as a separate experiment. [v112 cohort and optical inputs](SOLAR_SECONDARY_BLIND_SENTRY_V112.md) · [Published same-worker protocol](SOLAR_MODERN_ARM_PILOT_V110.md)
3. **Measure both stages on matched inputs.** Report RA-1 and RA-5 alongside native correspondence counts and spatial support. Report candidate-bank recall, raw top-one pose success over all eligible positives, wrong and missing positive poses, raw nonoverlap outputs, and complete same-worker latency and memory. Separate offline map work. Use paired terrain-group uncertainty for the larger cohort and account for both endpoints of a negative. Resume atlas ranking only when the banks contain correct candidates and the preregistered reference support gate passes.
