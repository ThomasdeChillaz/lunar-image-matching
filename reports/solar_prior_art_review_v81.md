Targeted prior-art review — 4 November 2026

The implemented three-module matcher is an unmeasured research candidate. Its trained checkpoints exist, but this review has no camera-pose results for the candidate. None of the numbers below ranks it against a published method. This bounded primary-source review does not establish novelty, publication quality, or likely interest from any particular researcher.

1. Closest published comparisons and what their numbers mean

LIMA, AIAA SciTech 2025, Rothenberger, Georgakis, Cheng and Ansar.
Full author-hosted paper inspected (Sections III–IV; Table 1). Correlation LIMA combines two already co-registered reference images and known Sun vectors to align a third, using a Lambertian slope-variance derivation and orthoprojected translations. Our single-reference-plus-DEM pose/Sun search has different inputs; that does not establish novelty. The paper separately adopts 11.5M-parameter LoFTR and explicitly discusses perspective images and 6DoF differences: general 6DoF matching is not our invention.

LoFTR fine-tuning uses 150×150km orthographic maps at 10m/px, elevations 0–4 degrees/1-degree increments, azimuths 0–330/30-degree increments, and eight anchor lights (elevations 2/4; azimuths 0/90/180/270); observations are unconstrained within the rendered set. Ground-truth training matches exclude large shadows. On unseen 0E, Table 1 RA-1/RA-5 are 70.6/85.9% for fine-tuned LoFTR, 20.8/39.1% off-the-shelf LoFTR, and 13.8/20.2% SIFT. These are returned-match percentages below 1/5px, not absolute camera-pose recovery; real LROC results are qualitative. The author's 2026 follow-up summary already targets illumination-matched maps.
Sources: https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2025-2073 ; https://ggeorgak11.github.io/ (public PDF link); https://noahzegna.github.io/ (2026 author summary).

LoMa rotation study / LoMa-R, CVPR Workshops 2026.
In Who Handles Orientation?, Table 3, RotDesc&Match scores 47.7 AUC@10px on SatAst; RotMatch scores 42.4 and NoRot 18.8. SatAst contains 39 original astronaut/satellite pairs and quarter-turn variants, evaluated through fitted homographies and manual correspondence errors. The paper uses at most 4,096 keypoints. These are not lunar camera-pose success rates. The official repository describes released LoMa-R as a rotation-invariant descriptor and matcher learned through augmentation. Exact correspondence between a released checkpoint and a paper configuration still belongs in the pinned integration record. Do not substitute LoMa-G or LoMa-B results for LoMa-R. The Table 3 caption's degree notation conflicts with Section 4.3, which explicitly specifies pixel homography error; use the latter definition and disclose the inconsistency if reproducing that table.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11809 ; https://github.com/davnords/LoMa

RoMa v2, inspected arXiv v3, July 2026.
Table 4 reports relative-pose AUC@20 degrees of 86.6 on MegaDepth-1500 and 73.8 on ScanNet-1500, with 800px coarse and 1,024px fine inference. It already contains subpixel refinement and predicted covariance; these are not candidate novelties. Its Table 8 throughput of 30.9 pairs/s and 4.8GB memory uses an H200, batch eight, 640px inputs and a custom CUDA kernel. That number cannot establish our two-thread CPU speed or memory advantage.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15706 ; https://github.com/Parskatt/RoMaV2

RoMa-Omega, September 2026 preprint.
Table 2 reports WxBS mAA@10px 72.9 versus RoMa v2 64.8 and original RoMa 72.6 in the same paper's evaluation. Its gain over original RoMa is therefore small on this particular benchmark. The method uses selected frozen VGGT-Omega features inside a matching pipeline; extracting 3D features alone is not its final method. Table 5 reports 1,033M total / 125M trainable parameters and 12.1 pairs/s, 29.9GB, on an A100 at 560px and batch eight. These are protocol-specific reference points, not deployment measurements for our setup. The v2 paper's own WxBS figure differs; do not combine the two evaluations into one ranking.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09507v1 ; https://github.com/davnords/RoMa-Omega

MOONSt3R / StereoLunar, ICCV Workshops 2025.
On 3,000 new simulated stereo pairs using the same LOLA 5m DEM and interpolated altitudes, Table 3 reports dynamic-case rotation accuracy below 2 degrees of 98.0%, and translation-DIRECTION accuracy below 2 degrees of 91.7%; pretrained MASt3R gives 90.1% and 76.5%. These are separate marginal metrics, not their joint rate or absolute metric translation accuracy. The paper's real descent-image study is qualitative. Lunar adaptation, physically rendered supervision and dense matching/geometry are already established here. Our known-reference-map PnP task and explicit lighting variables are different, but that difference is not evidence of superiority.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18172 ; https://github.com/clementinegrethen/StereoLunar

Geometric Foundation Model Distillation for Efficient Lunar 3D Reconstruction, July 2026 / official ECCV 2026 repository.
This is a particularly close antecedent for broad efficiency/distillation claims. On 1,200 StereoLunar pairs, Table 1 gives the 94.9M-parameter S3 student VCRE precision@5% image diagonal of 79.2%, versus 98.6% for the 688.6M teacher; its best student S2 has 154.9M parameters and 82.8%. Reconstruction errors use an optimal RANSAC similarity alignment to ground truth, recovering metric scale. Table 2 measures inference on an A100. The method distills geometry, cross-view descriptors and intermediate features. It does not establish our known-map pose metric, but efficient lunar learned models and teacher distillation are already published. Our frozen teacher is our compact descriptor, not MOONSt3R or another foundation model.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01851 ; https://github.com/clementinegrethen/mast3r-distillation

Visual SLAM with DEM Anchoring for Lunar Surface Navigation, March 2026 preprint.
This combines pretrained SuperPoint/LightGlue with DEM height/normal factors. On a 1.03km Mt. Etna loop, it reports aligned trajectory ATE RMSE of 21.43m versus 94.01m without DEM constraints, with the same ground-truth initialization. It also tests DEM uncertainty. Learned matching plus terrain physics and map uncertainty is therefore not a new general concept. This is sequential stereo SLAM and trajectory error, not independent changed-light camera-pair localization. Its LuSNAR DEMs derive from trajectory-aligned LiDAR, an optimistic condition the authors disclose.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17229

Geometry-aided Vision-based Localization of Future Mars Helicopters in Challenging Illumination Conditions, Geo-LoFTR, v3 April 2026 / official author page lists ICRA 2026.
Sections III–IV describe learned fusion of orthographic map imagery and reference depth, then query-to-map matching and RANSAC-PnP. Geometry-aided changed-light known-map pose estimation is already demonstrated, not merely stereo reconstruction. The system searches a 1km² area through overlapping 1,024×768 map windows. Section IV-A uses 500 nadir queries at 64–200m altitude, Sun elevation 10 degrees, and changing map azimuth: reported localization within 1m is 54–63%. This is a Mars position threshold, not our lunar joint rotation/relative-range pose criterion. Section IV-D reports 887±27ms for Geo-LoFTR versus 543±3ms LoFTR over 100 forwards on Jetson AGX Xavier, not our CPU or complete search-area latency. Real Mars descent validation has no significant illumination change and establishes no accuracy bound.

Official code and 824MB pretrained-weight archive are listed; code LICENSE and model card declare Apache-2.0. Availability is metadata-verified only. Optional consideration follows the existing required comparisons and compute admission; no integration is required by this review. Its orthographic-map/depth inputs need qualified coordinate handling for our reference setup. Dependency URLs, versions and access limits are in GEO_LOFTR_METADATA.json.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09795v3 ; https://github.com/nasa-jpl/mbl_mars ; https://huggingface.co/dpisanti/mbl-mars ; https://dpisanti.github.io/geo-loftr/ (officially linked; page fetch unavailable).

2. What is actually implemented, and what might distinguish it

A — one shared Sun hypothesis constrains correspondence assignment and pose.
Shadow-based lunar localization predates this study: Kaufmann et al. 2015 use known Sun, segmented shadows, DEM-rendered references and RANSAC. Their institutional abstract reports greater than 90% correct shadow matches in shadow-rich simulated landing sites and position error at most 1% of range; its tests include up to 400m position and 50min time drift. This is not a reusable guarantee for our cases. SUNDIAL jointly estimates satellite-scene geometry and Sun directions using secondary shadow rays, including an unknown-initial-Sun setting. Joint lighting/geometry inference is established.
Sources: https://elib.dlr.de/97110/ ; https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16215v1

Our narrower hypothesis is that one global illumination variable can eliminate mutually inconsistent choices in a sparse ambiguous correspondence graph, while alternating with camera pose and native patch measurements against fixed reference terrain. The solver is discrete/iterative physical inference, not an end-to-end learned joint network. The currently inspected graph has 256 anchors and eight query alternatives per anchor. A claim about the specific assignment constraint requires solar-off and per-correspondence-independent-Sun controls with identical candidate and pose budgets. A supplied-Sun track matters: ephemerides may already provide illumination in an operational navigation scenario. More latent variables can hurt identifiability and compute even if they sound physically appealing. Count actual informative, spatially distributed observations; many compatible shadow predictions need not distinguish poses or Sun angles.

B — counterfactual descriptor response, rather than only invariance.
Spherical-harmonic light transfer is established by precomputed radiance transfer; learned geometry/direction-conditioned light response is established by NRTF. Distilled Feature Fields already learn feature fields from pretrained image descriptors. These works target radiance or semantics, not this exact compact local matching task. They rule out broad claims that an SH lighting field, direction conditioning, or feature distillation is itself new.
Sources: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/precomputed-radiance-transfer-real-time-rendering-dynamic-low-frequency-lighting-environments/ ; https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/2022-NRTF/data/paper.pdf ; https://pfnet-research.github.io/distilled-feature-fields/

The candidate hypothesis is more specific: from a canonical 33px patch and observed-Sun/normal/trial-view conditioning, predict a frozen 32D local matcher's response at another Sun, then compare both images in that shared target-light descriptor space. The inspected model has 95,808 total parameters, including its frozen coarse encoder, and 67,536 trainable parameters. It predicts 36 degree-five SH coefficient vectors; it is an approximation, not exact equivariance, a physical BRDF, or a learned reliability/no-match head. Its pooled control retains the same architecture and full counterfactual reconstruction loss but pools angular response for the matching/discrimination use. Describe that precise control rather than calling it a separately designed generic invariant network.

The strongest limitation is identifiability: each point has at most 12 training target lights on one +1.5-degree subsolar-latitude circle. Degree-five SH restricted to one circle spans at most 11 Fourier modes, so those observations cannot identify 36 arbitrary coefficients per descriptor channel. The regularizer selects an extension outside the sampled path. Odd-hour-angle performance can test interpolation along the rendered path; it cannot demonstrate learned latitude or full-sphere generalization. Cast-shadow discontinuities and nonlinear descriptor changes further weaken a smooth low-order response assumption. A benefit must survive pose/canonicalization error and appear in correspondence/pose metrics, not teacher reconstruction loss alone.

C — sequential patch searches under competing pose/Sun hypotheses.
Active Matching, ECCV 2008, already chooses sequential image searches by expected Shannon information gain, maintains competing hypotheses, and updates them before further image processing. DaD 2025 learns diverse keypoint selection; adaptive selection alone is not new. The potential distinction is our particular robust disagreement score over pose/Sun predictions and seven declared height shifts, used to defer native feature extraction.
Sources: https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~ajd/Publications/chli_davison_eccv2008.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07347

The inspected policy is a weighted interval-separation heuristic over projected geometry, solar states and a geometric-consistency proxy. It does not predict learned descriptor observations for acquisition, compute expected information gain, or maintain a calibrated Bayesian posterior. If hypotheses have no live pose or all scores vanish, it falls back to uniform selection. Thus “active learned observation acquisition” would overstate this implementation. Fixed height shifts are limited sensitivity tests, not robustness to arbitrary DEM error. Evidence requires actual nonuniform decisions, same 32/64/128 search budgets, and complete runtime against uniform, strongest-appearance and geometry-only controls.

3. Falsification and interpretation requirements for the existing study

Use all eight A/B/C configurations and the pooled control on identical eligible cases, common final reference geometry/PnP, matched proposal-angle and initialization policies, and matched native search budgets. Keep the deterministic refinement/quantization correction as an engineering control. Complete the already-required qualified pretrained and direct render-and-match comparisons when their controls and budget permit; an internal ablation victory alone is not state of the art. Attribute a learned effect only if B actually computes responses: report response measurements, refresh calls, initial/live hypotheses, uniform fallbacks and failures as well as pose accuracy. If initialization prevents learned evidence from being used, an all-module label alone proves nothing about B.

For A, compare shared, solar-off and independent-Sun assignments; report Sun error and ambiguity separately from pose error. For B, compare SH and pooled with the same targets, capacity and training budget; held-out lights remain unavailable during training. For C, show decision differences and pose/time gains at matched budgets, including declared height perturbations. A positive outcome from only the integrated system supports an interaction hypothesis, not three independently established contributions.

All eligible positive cases belong in the denominator, including failed estimates. Include wrong poses and nonoverlap acceptance, with both negative terrain endpoints recorded. Use terrain-group paired intervals, not repeated views as independent geography. Training seed variation and renderer/DEM dependence remain limitations. A later frozen reserve is needed for a final generalization claim; this review does not authorize opening it.

Efficiency must account for more than network size. The current full generic reference table contains 1,627 Sun directions × 256 anchors × seven height variants, plus geometry and other map assets. Four measured v80 reference preparations took 108.5–145.9 seconds each; the inspected u012 compressed archive alone is 1,931,596 bytes. These are preparation/storage measurements, not pose results. Report offline preparation, map storage, cold setup, warm inference and any online physical lookups separately. For Q queries per reference, disclose amortized time as preparation/Q plus online cost. Image-only baselines do not require a Sun catalogue. Known-map comparisons must give geometry-aware baselines equivalent reference information. Separate CPU/hardware strata and avoid calling a process-lifetime RSS high-water mark per-model memory. No neural-parameter ratio is a quality or speed ranking.

4. Defensible framing before results exist

“We evaluate whether shared illumination constraints, compact counterfactual descriptor responses and height-sensitive patch acquisition improve known-map lunar camera localization under changed lighting and viewpoint.”

The research contribution, if established, would be a measured accuracy/compute tradeoff and a causal explanation from these controls. A useful negative result could instead identify uninformative solar evidence, an inadequately identified lighting representation, or acquisition overhead exceeding its benefit. None should be renamed a breakthrough. A broader systematic literature search and actual matched pose results remain necessary before a novelty or superiority claim.
