The three implemented mechanisms have substantial prior art. The contribution worth testing is their specific, compact lunar-localization implementation and its measured benefit. This targeted review does not establish novelty, exclusivity or superiority. It checked primary papers, author pages and official research repositories on 4 November 2026, starting from `reports/REQUIREMENTS_AND_SOTA_V69.md`.

1. Shared Sun, correspondences and pose.

   Shadow-based lunar localization is established. [Kaufmann et al., 2015](https://elib.dlr.de/97110/) match segmented shadow configurations against DEM-rendered references using known Sun position and RANSAC. [Wehrwein et al., 2015](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/projects/shadows/) estimate shadows and Sun direction from reconstructed photo collections. [SUNDIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16215v1), Sections 3.4 and 4.3, jointly estimates scene geometry and image-dependent Sun directions using shadow rays during satellite-scene training.

   Ansar's coauthored [2025 LIMA work](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2025-2073) studies correlation matching and learned illumination-invariant features; the [2026 follow-up](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-2244) synthesizes illumination-matched maps. Their [author summaries](https://noahzegna.github.io/) were accessible; full papers were not, so this review does not infer uninspected algorithm details or numerical comparisons.

   Our candidate distinction, which remains unproven: one query-Sun hypothesis constrains all sparse correspondence assignments while they alternate with native refinement and camera pose against a fixed reference map. This differs in task and execution from known-Sun shadow localization, multi-image Sun estimation and per-scene neural reconstruction. It does not make joint lighting/geometry reasoning new.

   Decisive evidence: identical candidate/pose budgets for shared-Sun, solar-off and independent-Sun controls; a supplied-Sun track; pose benefit on changed-light cases with Sun error and support reported separately. Unknown Sun is not automatically the operationally preferable assumption when ephemerides are available.

2. Learned SH descriptor responses and counterfactual teacher targets.

   [Sloan, Kautz and Snyder, 2002](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/precomputed-radiance-transfer-real-time-rendering-dynamic-low-frequency-lighting-environments/) already encode light transfer with spherical harmonics, including shadowing and interreflection under their low-frequency setting. [NRTF, ECCV 2022](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/2022-NRTF/data/paper.pdf), Section 3, learns radiance transfer conditioned on geometry and incoming/outgoing directions using synthetic one-light-at-a-time and real-image supervision. [Distilled Feature Fields, NeurIPS 2022](https://pfnet-research.github.io/distilled-feature-fields/) distills pretrained 2D feature extractors into a 3D feature field for semantic editing.

   Our candidate distinction, not a novelty finding: predict a frozen local matcher's 32D descriptor at counterfactual Sun directions from canonical image patches, using 36 SH coefficient vectors, observed-light/normal/view conditioning, and a combined model below 100,000 parameters. Outputs serve correspondence selection and absolute pose, rather than RGB relighting or semantic editing. The frozen teacher is our original compact descriptor; this is not evidence of compressing a foundation model.

   [Basri and Jacobs, 2003](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~djacobs/pubs_files/Basri%20Jacobs%20-%20Lambertian%20Reflectance%20and%20Linear%20Subspaces.pdf), Section 2, explicitly excludes cast shadows from its convex-Lambertian analysis. Its low-dimensional illumination result therefore does not guarantee accurate degree-five responses for nonconvex terrain or nonlinear CNN descriptors. Our training lights also do not identify an arbitrary full-sphere response; the measured angular domain must bound the claim.

   Decisive evidence: capacity/data/budget-matched pooled response; unchanged frozen coarse/native foundation; held-out physical lights; correspondence and pose gains beyond deterministic refinement. Report whether learned responses were actually used, coefficient instability and failures near hard shadow transitions.

3. Active, height-robust patch acquisition.

   [Chli and Davison, Active Matching, ECCV 2008](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~ajd/Publications/chli_davison_eccv2008.pdf), Sections 2–3, already performs sequential feature searches under multiple hypotheses, choosing measurements by expected Shannon information gain and updating consensus before further image processing. [Olson, 2002](https://faculty.washington.edu/cfolson/abstracts/ar2002.html) selects natural-terrain localization landmarks using predicted uncertainty. [Fang's 2020 lunar-navigation thesis](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/terrain-relative-navigation-for-lunar-polar-roving-exploiting-geometry-shadows-and-planning) combines map/sensor uncertainty, shadows, localization-uncertainty prediction and information-aware planning.

   Our candidate distinction, unproven: select native patch searches whose pose/Sun predictions stay separated across seven specified terrain-height variants, deferring native feature extraction until selected. The implemented score is weighted interval separation of projected geometry, solar-shadow states and a geometric-consistency proxy. It is a heuristic, not expected Shannon information gain, and currently does not predict learned descriptor observations for the acquisition score. Global height shifts are a limited uncertainty model, not a guarantee against arbitrary DEM error.

   Decisive evidence: same 32/64/128 measurement budgets against uniform, strongest appearance and geometry-only selection; actual nonuniform decisions and response usage; complete CPU time including physics/hypothesis management; pose gains surviving the declared height shifts. It cannot claim superiority merely by visiting fewer patches.

Suggested paper wording: “We study a compact lunar matching pipeline that couples a shared illumination hypothesis, learned descriptor-response prediction and uncertainty-aware patch acquisition. These components adapt established shadow-localization, radiance-transfer/distillation and active-matching ideas. Matched ablations test whether their specific combination improves camera localization under changing illumination and viewpoint.”

Access limits: DLR, Fang and the 2025/2026 AIAA items were checked through institutional abstracts or author summaries; NRTF, Active Matching, Basri–Jacobs and SUNDIAL methods were inspected directly. No claimed absence of an exact antecedent follows from this limited review. No model evaluation, training, repository edit or publication was performed.
