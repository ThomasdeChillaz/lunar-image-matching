# Test whether coarse radiance can rerank blind lunar pose hypotheses

The v110 diagnostic found a coherent 32-pixel pattern in one low-light simulated pair at the known geometry and physical Sun direction. It also found much weaker native-pixel agreement. The next test asks whether a terrain-scattered score can help choose among plausible poses when the geometry and Sun direction have to be inferred. [Cross-view evidence](SOLAR_RAW_CROSSVIEW_V110.md)

The isolated ranker already accepts an observed query image, candidate-predicted detector images, reference-derived coverage and the original candidate order. Constructed fixtures verified positive gain and offset invariance, deterministic ties, exact fallback for inadequate support, and the gradient-removal control. The predictor that renders candidate irradiance into the detector and the fresh lunar ranking test are pending. - Retain all scheduled positive pairs and both terrain endpoints of fixed nonoverlap cases. Separate a known-Sun diagnostic from the primary estimated-Sun track.
- Use the current blind proposal generator and its reference map, reference/query images and query intrinsics. Freeze its existing pose and Sun proposal budget. If it returns fewer candidates or misses the correct pose, record that outcome. Do not insert a true pose, true Sun, or a perturbation of truth. Freeze or hash the candidate list before computing the new rank. The same poses and Sun hypotheses go to all ranking methods.

## A scoring rule that exposes ambiguity

1. For each surviving pose and Sun, use the reference-side terrain and irradiance atlas to predict a coarse detector image. Visibility comes from forward projection and a candidate-specific terrain z-buffer. The only query pixels used for ranking are the observed image. The ranker receives no query depth, true pose, Sun labels, or true visibility mask.
2. Sample means on a fixed 32-pixel query grid, with 16 pixels declared as a sensitivity. Compare hypotheses on the intersection of available atlas/terrain support in the same detector cells. Require support in at least three fixed detector quadrants and a declared minimum number of cells per supported quadrant. A coverage failure or insufficient variation returns the existing ranking and an explicit reason.
3. Remove a single scene-wide additive offset and positive multiplicative gain when scoring appearance. Score signed correlation after centering the block signals. Compute candidate-wise cell errors and average robustly with equal quadrant weight so one large lit patch does not dominate. Fix one broad-gradient control that removes an affine detector-plane trend on the same cells, then compares the residual pattern. A gain confined to the raw trend is evidence of poor pose specificity.
4. Preserve the original blind ranking as the principal comparator. Use uniform-scattered coarse cells and the affine trend alone as attribution controls. Keep fixed query-offset wrong-pose comparisons as sensitivity checks if desired. They stay outside the candidate ranking and receive no credit as localization outcomes.

## Measure candidate coverage separately from ranking

After writing the complete blinded rankings, use sealed query truth to score them. Apply the historical pose criterion of at most 2° rotation error and at most 1% of the median reference range in translation. Report correct top-1 poses divided by all eligible positives, candidate-oracle coverage, correct-pose rank on covered cases, and counts of ranking wins and losses relative to the original score. Separate wrong poses, nonoverlap proposals, and unavailable predictions. Do not choose rejection thresholds on these cases.

Run the fixed ranking on at least two independent renderer seeds per declared dark condition when available. Report seed agreement and paired terrain-component intervals where the number of development groups permits. Inspect where atlas support, raw signal amplitude or pose/Sun ambiguity leaves no separable ranking. A useful result improves top-1 recovery on changed-light development cases with the same candidates, retains the gain across seeds, and improves more than the broad-gradient control. A high candidate-oracle ceiling with weak rank gains points to the score. Low coverage points to the blind proposal generator. Report either outcome before asking to freeze any final test.
